# $100M equity ambition: operating-scale sensitivities

The target is an ambition, not a present valuation, a promised return or an outcome predicted by the model. No independent company valuation has been obtained. `valuationTargets()` computes equity target + net debt = target enterprise value. Negative net debt represents excess cash. Acquisition fees, tax, control/illiquidity discounts and dilution require separate analysis.

Default sensitivity inputs are 1.5× nonrecurring hardware revenue, 6× software ARR, 10× EBITDA and 20% acquisition premium. These are intentionally editable assumptions, not observed trading multiples or broker quotes. At zero net debt, $100m implies approximately $66.7m hardware revenue, $16.7m ARR, or $10m EBITDA. The hybrid splits EV equally: ~$33.3m hardware revenue plus ~$8.3m software ARR, counting distinct streams only. A 20% assumed acquisition premium implies ~$83.3m standalone EV; it creates no actual buyer interest.

A true public-multiple comparison needs same-date market capitalization, fully diluted shares, debt/cash, lease treatment, LTM revenue, recurring revenue definitions and comparable margins/geographies. A private target also needs discounts for scale, concentration, warranty liability and governance. Those inputs are not verified here, so the interface presents sensitivity math instead of fabricated market multiples.

## Evidence investors would need

Safe certified installations; permissioned calibrated telemetry; realized customer economics; repeatable gross/contribution margins after installation/warranty/CAC; renewal cohorts and service costs; audited revenue recognition; cash discipline and supply agreements; defensible IP/data rights; diversified partners and customers. Strategic acquisition discussions require an actual process and qualified advisers. Any equity raise changes founder ownership and must be modeled separately from enterprise value.
