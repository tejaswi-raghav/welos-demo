# Competition and defensibility

| Alternative | Existing strength | Welos must prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inverter-vendor monitoring | Native generation telemetry and installed base | Incremental value across vendors beyond an existing free app |
| Local EPC with O&M service | Utility access, installation and customer trust | Better diagnosis/workflow without duplicating EPC responsibilities |
| Facility/BMS platforms | Multi-site operations and controls | Affordable integration and actionable single-site evidence |
| Energy-management SaaS | Data workflows and recurring business model | Actual retention, integration reliability and cost to serve |
| Manual inspection / cleaning | Low upfront software spend | Measured net benefit after water/energy/labor costs |

SolarEdge and Enphase are relevant equipment/platform comparables, not proof that Welos has equivalent scale, certification or margins. ABB's Sensorfact acquisition shows strategic interest in energy-management SaaS, but the announcement provides no transaction multiple for Welos. No unsupported competitor market-share/pricing table is used.

A defensible asset would be a permissioned, calibrated history linking faults, interventions and subsequent energy outcomes across repeatable installations. Achieving it requires sound data rights, device identity, measurement controls, service discipline and customer renewals. The current deterministic demo alone is easy to reproduce and is not a moat.
