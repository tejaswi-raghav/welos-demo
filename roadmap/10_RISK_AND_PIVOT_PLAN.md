# Stop and redesign criteria

Assessment: 27 November 2026.

| Trigger | Decision | Lower-capital fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer than eight documented incidents across 30 interviews, or fewer than three qualified pilots | Stop bespoke bench expansion | Sell a manual service audit or study a different EPC segment |
| No incremental accuracy or time saved over incumbent workflow across 20 labeled windows | Pause automated diagnostic claim | Dashboard-independent service handoff and report automation |
| Safety gate fails or reference uncertainty exceeds acceptance | Suspend bench testing | Approved lab support and logged replay |
| Landed BOM with reserve exceeds AED 10,000 | Block orders | Borrow approved equipment or defer optional hardware |
| Forecast customer benefit fails to cover approved purchase/service cost | Reprice and reduce scope | EPC-only diagnostics from existing data |
| Persistent negative unit contribution after real support and warranty | Stop geographic expansion | License workflow or charge per service event |
| Recurring payment refused in two cohorts | Re-test proposal | One-time audit or bundled EPC fee |
| Installer access or local permissions unresolved | Pause site work | Permissioned exported reports and supervised tests |
| Wet-loop benefit cannot beat its installed and maintenance cost | Keep dry scope | Independent manual cleaning assessment |

Three critical reviews were conducted as analytical passes, not external approvals. Engineering flagged observed accuracy, calibration and supervision as release gates. Investor review removed implied market share and anchored the forecast to paid cohorts. Execution review moved rinse out of the first build, staged spending and limited the first launch to one installer channel. Unresolved: actual funding, professional review, OEM data access and buyer willingness to pay.
